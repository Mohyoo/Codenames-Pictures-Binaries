- First, go to the 'node.js' folder, and enter the folder that represents
  your operating system.

- You'll find the 'node' executable and the 'run' script.

 Linux, Mac, and AIX users will also find the 'setup' script, which must be
  executed once to allow execution permission for the 'node' executable and the
  'run' script. Simply open the terminal in the directory where the 'setup'
  script is, and run this command:
  - Linux/Mac: sh setup.sh
  - AIX:ksh setup.sh
  After that, you'll no longer need the 'setup' script anymore; next time just
  double click the 'run' script directly.

- Double click the 'run' script to launch the game server.

 AIX users can start the server anytime by entering './run.sh' in the terminal.

- Because the app does not sync across devices, your only option is:
  1. Single Device: Play entirely on one screen (like a laptop or TV).      
	 Operatives look at the screen to guess. The Spymaster looks at the hidden
	 key card on the web page, a piece of paper, their memory, or a completely
	 separate screen (Operators should not look when the Spy is looking in the
	 key card board), then right-clicks the main screen to reveal the cards.

* The methods listed below have issues, such as sync lack (revealing cards,
  adding players, and other changes won't appear to other devices; also when you
  open the link http://host_ip_address:3000/#/play in other devices, the board
  cards will change sadly):
  1. Spies in one device, Operators in another device: Though limited, since
     there is no sync for the changes, but it's still an option.
  2. Everyone on their device: Same as above.
  3. Play the way you want: There is no restriction to the way you and your
     friends/siblings can play, so find what fits your desire.
  * Unless you find a workaround, you may not be able to play that way.
  I apologize for the inconvenience (though it's by the author, not me hehe).

  * And in case you choose to play in the issue-prone methods, you can get the
    host IP address this way:
    the host opens their terminal/command promptand type:
    - Windows: 'ipconfig' then look for the IPv4 Address.
    - Linux/Mac: 'ifconfig' then look for the inet or inet6 Address.
    The host can simply play by typing 'http://localhost:3000/#/play' in their
    browser. While others should type 'http://host_ip_address:3000/#/play'
    (change 'host_ip_address' to the host's IP. For example:
    'http://192.168.0.67:3000/#/play')
    The guests (other players) can play simply by opening their browser and
    typing the invite link. Any device (including phone) should work.
    (Of cource, the host and the guests must be both connected to the same Wi-Fi
     network).

* For more fun (or in case of issues), you can use a paper, or quick notes apps
  to keep the game records.
